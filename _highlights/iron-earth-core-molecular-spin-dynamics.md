Ordinary molecular dynamics treats atoms as classical particles and leaves magnetic moments out, even though magnetism affects phase stability and mechanical response. The authors build a molecular-spin dynamics model for iron in which atomic spins evolve alongside the ionic degrees of freedom, with longitudinal spin fluctuations treated explicitly. The model is trained on first-principles data and aimed at the pressure and temperature conditions of Earth’s core.

The simulations recover the metastable body-centered-cubic phase of iron above 240 GPa and reproduce compressional wave velocities, bulk moduli, viscosity and electrical conductivity. By coupling the spin dynamics to time-dependent density functional theory, the authors also obtain electronic transport properties. These results help investigate the material behaviour relevant to the geodynamo and to iron phases near the melting curve.
